# Habits

## First things first

The first thing you should do before starting you work is review what's done and
what need to be done.

This way you can plan your time accordingly.

## Reflect

A small, fast "solo-Hansei" per se. Reflect on why something worked or broke and
improve upon it.

## Keep a journal

Use it for you planning, reflections, schedules, task, ideas, etc

## Take breaks

Clarity beats speeds.

Having frequent breaks is important to avoid exhaustion, frustration,
tunnel-vision, etc.

Using the pomodoro technique and alternatives might help:

### Pomodoro Technique

1. Work 25/50/120 minutes
2. Break for 5/10/27 minutes
3. Repeat steps 1-2 four times, then take a long break, 30/60 minutes.
4. Repeate steps 1-3 until the task is done.
5. Review task just completed (optional)
6. Reflect on what did you learn.(optional)
7. Review next tasks and plan accordingly

Sometimes 25 minutes is too little to work on something, that's why there's
other times in steps 1, 2 and 3 (the 60-minute long break is for the 100-minute
work)

### Time-Tracking

This MUST be individual choice and NOT work/corporate IMPOSED.

You want to track your time spent on various activities, tasks or projects
throughout the day, and a company having access to it will just give them
excuses to misinterpret the way they want and exploit you.

Which will have the inverse effect that we want.

When done as a personal choice it helps to increase awareness of how you spend
your time, identify areas that can be improved and optimized for efficiency and
productivity.

1. Choose a time tracking method (your phone's timer, an app, etc)
2. Define start and end times for the task/activity you're going to do
3. Be specific
4. After a few days/weeks, analyze your data, identify patterns and improve upon
   it
5. Make adjustments

### Task-batching

If you (think you) can't do one thing at a time, try task-batching.

1. Identify similar tasks, group them by similarity or relatedness
2. Set a starting and end time for this task group
3. Focus on only one group of tasks at a time
4. Work on each task within the group and try to complete as many as you can
   within your defined time.
5. Take a break after completig each batch.

### Eat-the-frog

From Mark Twain’s quote: “Eat a live frog first thing in the morning, and
nothing worse will happen to you the rest of the day.”

This thought process can be translated to pretty much anything you want to.

In this case is coding.

1. See what needs to be done, most important/challenging/worst one is the "frog"
2. Once you identified the frog, start working on it. Move on when you're done
3. Pick the next frog

### Eisenhower Matrix or 4 Quadrants of Time Management

Popularized by the 34th US President, Dwight D. Eisenhower. If prioritizes
efficient and quick decisions.

The matrix categorizes tasks into 4 quadrants based on two criteria: Urgency and
Importance.

1st Quadrant: Urgent and Important (Do first). Tasks in this quadrant are urgent
and important and require immediate attention, these tasks are required to
achieve the goals set and have deadlines.

2nd Quadrant: Important but not Urgent (Schedule). Since it's important but not
urgent, it's required to achieve long-term goals but can be scheduled to be
worked on at a later date.

3rd Quadrant: Urgent bot not Important (Delegate). These tasks are urgent but
does not contribute directly to the long-term goal. They're distractions and
consumes your energy better spent elsewhere. Try to delegate/automate them as
much as possible.

4th Quadrant: Not urgent and not Important (Eliminate). They do not contribute
to your goal and isn't urgent, usually just wastes your time. Engaging in
activities in this quadrant can prevent you from focusing on what trully
matters. Minimize these tasks or eliminate all together whenever possible.

### The 80/20 Rule (Pareto Principle)

Pareto's principle suggests that 80% of results comes from a 20% input or
efforts.

Translating this to code, 80% of your productivity may be a result of 20% of
your focus on high-value tasks.

With this in mind, you should identify high-priority tasks that can improve your
productivity and focus on them.

By focusing on important few, you can optimize your time and resources to
achieve better results more efficiently.

Specific ratios may vary 80/20 is not a hard rule, it may be 70/30, 90/10, etc.

### Energy Mapping Strategy

Our energy fluctuates throughout the day, this strategy is tailors your schedule
to align with your natural rythm.

Reseve challenging, demanding, etc for when your energy peaks and tackle
less demanding tasks during your low-energy moments.

By synchronizing your work to your body's natural flow, you optimize your
productivity and prevents stress and burnout.
